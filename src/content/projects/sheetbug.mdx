## what it does

sheetbug turns "who can help?" into a link people can actually act on. an
organizer makes a sheet of dates and slots, sets how many spots each one has,
and shares the link or a downloadable qr code. participants pick an open spot
on their phone without creating an account.

it works for school volunteers, snack rotations, conference appointments,
potlucks, and shifts. dates can repeat, an afternoon can be split into time
slots, or a sheet can have no dates at all. the public page can group entries
by date or by slot.

- confirmation emails include a calendar file and a link to change or cancel
  the sign-up
- reminders use the sheet's time zone, with a per-sheet opt-out
- organizers can upload a header image and pictures for individual slots
- the dashboard shows drafts, active sheets, and past sheets, with a next-date
  panel, filled counts, favorites, and actions to duplicate or archive a sheet

the first version went live on november 2, 2026. the participant flow and the
organizer dashboard are working software, with more of the organizer settings
still to come.

## why i built it

i wanted to try building from a proven product's behavior instead of making up
a feature list as i went. sign-up sheets have a clear job: let an organizer
ask for help and let people claim spots without a back-and-forth email chain.
that gave me a concrete workflow to build and test.

sheetbug started as a clean-room experiment around that workflow, using public
documentation as a reference and making my own screens, wording, branding, and
code. the goal is to turn it into something real organizers keep using. getting
the basic flow working is the first part; giving people a reason to switch and
finding those users are still open questions.

## how it's built

- **next.js + react + typescript**: the organizer wizard, dashboard, and public
  sign-up pages, styled with tailwind css and shadcn/ui
- **better auth**: organizer accounts and optional participant accounts; a
  participant can also manage a sign-up through an emailed link
- **prisma + postgresql**: sheets, dates, slots, entries, and uploaded images.
  capacity is checked inside a transaction so simultaneous submissions can't
  overbook a slot
- **resend**: production confirmation and reminder emails, with bounce and
  complaint handling. local development mail stays in mailpit
- **railway**: the app and postgres, with reminders and data-retention jobs
  running inside the server
- **vitest + playwright**: database-backed tests and browser acceptance checks.
  github actions runs the verification command, and railway waits for ci
  before deploying

uploaded pictures are resized, converted to webp, and stripped of metadata.
the dashboard requests small thumbnails rather than loading full header images
into every row.

## what's next

templates, themes, and more organizer controls are in the planned feature
queue. the bigger question is which additions real organizers need first.
i'm keeping the first release focused on making a sheet, filling its spots,
and helping people remember what they signed up for.
