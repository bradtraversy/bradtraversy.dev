Kudoshelf now accepts video testimonials, including recording directly in
the browser. Cards use a still image until someone presses Play, rather
than loading every video as the page opens.

The awkward part was the difference between a file container and the codecs
inside it. A video called MP4 isn't a guarantee that a browser can play both
its picture and its sound. One HEVC sample played audio with no picture in
Chrome on Linux, so that format is refused. The form offers in-page recording
as the alternative.

Browser-recorded WebM needed attention too: the recordings had no duration
header. Adding the length before upload made the player usable. The file
checks also had to bound their reads and metadata counts, since an uploaded
file isn't something the parser can trust.

The feature landed on local main after three review rounds, with 325 unit
tests and 74 browser tests passing. The playback checks were in Chrome and
Firefox on Linux. Real Safari, iPhone, Android, and production storage checks
are still outstanding, so this isn't a claim of verified mobile support.
