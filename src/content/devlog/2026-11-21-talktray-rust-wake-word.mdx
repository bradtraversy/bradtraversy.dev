TalkTray is a planned tray app for starting an assistant's own voice mode
with a wake phrase. Before building the app, I wanted to know whether the
local keyword detector behaved the same in Rust as it did in my Python proof.

Using the same sherpa-onnx version, the Rust proof matched Python on all 3,528
clip-and-setting pairs, down to the frame where a hit was reported. It also
matched on a continuous audio file. That's a useful foundation for the Rust
core, not evidence that the whole tray application exists.

The more interesting result was the trade-off around recognition. Widening
the search picked up more wake phrases immediately after speech, but also
triggered on similar-sounding names. I kept the narrower search and made the
sensitivity choices change the detection threshold instead.

Even then, a wake phrase said without a pause after ordinary speech was often
missed. At the chosen search width, the proof detected 20 of 36 with no pause
and 30 of 36 with a one-second pause. Matching Python doesn't remove that
limitation; it tells me the port isn't introducing a different one.

These measurements came from Mac voices and a disposable proof. The actual
app and broader platform checks are still ahead.
