Rehearsium now runs its analysis after a conversation has been saved. It
transcribes the recording, measures the answers, and shows progress or a
retryable failure. The recording stays saved if analysis fails.

The tricky part is deciding what a pause means. Pressing Pause in the app
shouldn't count as hesitating during an answer. Speech on either side of
that deliberate break needs to be measured separately, without creating
a false long pause where the recording joins.

Word timestamps need care too. In one run, the transcription stretched
"yeah" backward across several seconds of silence. Using the word's end
time placed it more accurately for this measurement than its start time.

I checked three real conversations: Natural mode, Patient mode with long
pauses, and Patient mode with Pause pressed mid-sentence. The pause-and-resume
run kept one answer and didn't invent a long pause at the join. Reanalyzing
also left the existing word data untouched.

The verification suite passed 738 tests. That doesn't establish general
filler-word accuracy across speakers or prove a near-100-minute conversation.
The results page is later work; this slice gets the saved conversation
through analysis and makes a failure recoverable.
