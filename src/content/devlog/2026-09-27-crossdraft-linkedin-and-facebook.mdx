The last crossdraft entry ended with DEV and Hashnode working and a Substack
adapter marked experimental. Since then three more destinations landed, and
the rules around them matter more than the platform count.

LinkedIn first. `connect linkedin` walks through OAuth with your own developer
app: it prints the consent URL, listens on a loopback port for the callback,
verifies the account, and stores the token locally. Sharing takes a short
Markdown adaptation, not the article itself. The body becomes the feed text
and the canonical URL becomes the link card, and anything over LinkedIn's 3000
characters is rejected instead of silently cut. I used it live to share a
video post, then to edit that post in place. Editing keeps the post ID,
reactions, and comments, and it goes through the same confirmation, source
recheck, and durable edit journal as a publish. An edit never falls back to
creating a new post.

Facebook posts to one configured Page and nothing else: no personal profile,
no target selector, no fallback. `auth facebook` does a read-only lookup of
the Page ID and refuses a same-ID response that turns out to be a user rather
than a Page. Publishing shows the exact Page, the complete text, and the link,
then waits for `publish facebook` typed in an interactive terminal.

Substack's private-draft path got the same verify-and-record treatment as DEV
and Hashnode. It is still marked experimental until it passes a live-account
test, and publication still happens in Substack's editor.

The common thread is receipts. Every destination records an exclusive attempt
before the write, never retries a mutation on its own, and keeps uncertain
outcomes for inspection instead of guessing. Duplicate protection is keyed by
account plus canonical URL, so running a command twice updates rather than
double-posts. One lesson from LinkedIn: a 200 response is a receipt, not
proof of what is live. I checked the edited post in the browser before
trusting the journal.

Still a private project, still no release. Next is picking the next article
to run through all of it end to end.
