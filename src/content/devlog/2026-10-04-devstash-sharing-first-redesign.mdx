DevStash could already share collections and individual snippets, but the
app still felt built around storing things. I reworked the signed-in
experience around capturing something quickly and sharing it when you're ready.

Home now starts with a paste box and one filtered list. Paste detection
suggests the item type, but you can change the guess before saving. Dense
rows are the default, with a switch back to code cards. Sharing controls
sit together at the top of the item drawer, and a dedicated Shared page
shows what you've made available to others.

The lists also have pagination now, starting at 25 items with options for
50 or 100. That includes Favorites and Shared, not just the main item pages.

Alongside the app redesign, I shipped downloadable snippet images and
rewrote the public homepage around "Stash it. Share it." Rendering long
snippets exposed a performance problem: clipping a tall image panel made
render time grow dramatically. Removing that clipping brought a 160-line
render from over two minutes to about half a second in the local checks.

DevStash is also free for now. Pro is hidden, AI helpers are available to
everyone, and the old free-tier limits have been replaced with higher abuse
ceilings. New file and image items are disabled; existing attachments remain
readable and downloadable.

The changes are deployed. It remains the project from my Coding with AI
course, with the original course version preserved under the `course-final`
tag. The live app is continuing to develop beyond that version.
