Sheetbug is a sign-up sheet app I've been building since October 22. An
organizer makes a sheet of dates and slots, shares one link, and people claim
spots on their phone without making an account. Confirmation emails, change
links and a dashboard were already in. This week reminders went in, the last
feature I wanted before a first release.

What shipped:

- Every entry with an email gets one reminder at 8 AM in the sheet's time zone,
  the organizer's chosen lead time before the slot: 1 to 14 days, 2 by default.
- Anyone with an account can pick their own timing, which wins over the
  organizer's for their own sign-ups.
- One email per person per sheet per day, listing everything they signed up for
  that day, with a link to change it.
- Nothing goes out for drafts, archived sheets, entries without an email, or
  anyone who signed up inside the lead time, since their confirmation just
  arrived.

The runner is one function. Postgres picks the due entries in a single
statement, so two runners never share an entry:

```sql
UPDATE entry SET "reminderClaimedAt" = now()
WHERE id IN (
  SELECT e.id FROM entry e ...
  WHERE ... FOR UPDATE OF e SKIP LOCKED)
RETURNING id
```

Every timestamp in that query is made explicit, stored values read as UTC and
the due moment built in the sheet's zone, so the connection's time zone never
matters. The job runs from a one-minute timer inside the Next.js process or
from a POST route behind a bearer secret, so the hosting choice decides which.

The lesson came from going through the failure cases before merging. My first
version marked an entry sent at the moment it was claimed, so a run that died
between claiming and sending lost those reminders for good. It's now a lease:
claiming sets its own timestamp, the sent timestamp is written after each email
goes out, a run sends for at most 15 minutes and gives back whatever it still
holds, and a claim older than 30 minutes with no send is taken again. A crash
now means a late reminder, not a lost one. The numbers come from nodemailer's
10-minute socket timeout: half the lease leaves room for one last hung send.

The runner has 15 database-backed tests, including three runs at once against
six due entries, each sent exactly once, and an end-to-end test that triggers
the job and reads the email back out of Mailpit.

Next is the branding pass, then the first release.
