Our phones back up everything to Immich, and most of what arrives is a
screenshot somebody took to send in a text. Some of it is trash, some of it
is a receipt you want later, and mixed in with it are the photos that
actually matter. My wife wanted family albums. I wanted the timeline to stop
being a wall of iMessage screenshots.

So I wrote a small Python tool that runs on the homelab box once a day. Each
new upload goes through cheap rules first: a PNG with no camera EXIF at a
phone screen size is a screenshot, and Immich's own face recognition tells me
who is in the shot. Then one call to OpenAI's Luna model with a 768px
preview returns a label from a two-level taxonomy of 45 categories plus a
keep score from 0 to 3, in a strict JSON schema. The actions are boring on
purpose: hierarchical tags, screenshots and documents and junk archived out
of the timeline but still searchable, and anything scoring zero dropped into a
review album that I empty by hand. The tool has no delete path at all, and a
photo with a named face can never be called junk.

I dry-ran it over the 860 photos from one weekend before it was allowed to
write anything. Zero errors, about 1.7 million input tokens, and contact
sheets of the trash candidates all held up. Two things changed after seeing
the numbers. Favoriting was supposed to mark the best family photos and would
have starred 476 of 550, so it is off. And three "screenshots" turned out to
be real family photos saved from messages, so a screenshot with a family face
now stays in the timeline.

The albums turned out not to need the model at all. Immich had already run
face recognition across the whole 20-year library and I had named the four
of us long ago, so a faces-only pass filled a Family album with 17,000 photos
and the kids' albums with 9,000 each in a few minutes, for free. The one
wrinkle was that every gym mirror selfie of mine has my face in it, so they
landed in Family. The fix was an exclusion list plus a "sweep" command that
asks the model one yes or no question over the 514 solo photos of me and
files the yeses into the Gym album. It found five.

The bugs worth mentioning were all in the checkpoint that decides which
uploads are done. Two independent review passes found six of them: a
`--limit` flag that advanced the checkpoint past unscanned photos, an
"applied" set keyed by asset instead of by row, a checkpoint frozen forever by
one photo that failed and then got deleted, and socket errors that escaped a
worker and threw away a whole batch. The rule that survived is simple. The
checkpoint means the scanned window is done, it sits five minutes behind the
window's end, it never passes a photo that still needs work, and it never
moves backwards.

If you self-host Immich, the cheapest classifier you have is the face data it
already computed. Use that first and save the model calls for the ambiguous
stuff.
