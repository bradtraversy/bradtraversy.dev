DevStash now offers Google sign-in alongside GitHub. I also closed new
email-and-password registration, but kept password sign-in and recovery for
people who already use them.

Adding another button was the small part. The more important question was
what should happen when someone signs in with Google using an email that's
already attached to a DevStash account. Creating a second account would
leave their existing stash behind. Linking accounts without checking who
controls the email would be worse.

The new flow links by email only when Google reports that email as verified.
If the existing account never verified that address, the link marks it
verified and removes its old password. That prevents someone who registered
another person's address from keeping access through a password they set
before the real owner arrived. An already verified password account keeps
its password.

The review also tightened GitHub registration to require a verified email.
That matters because account linking depends on the meaning of "verified,"
not just whether a provider happened to return an email field.

I considered removing passwords altogether. But some existing users have no
linked Google or GitHub account. Removing their sign-in method, or its
forgot-password flow, would leave them without a working way back in.

So new accounts use Google or GitHub. Existing password users can reveal the
email form through a small link on the sign-in page, and password recovery
still works. Closing one registration path doesn't have to strand the people
who used it before.

Both changes are merged and deployed. Tests and local browser checks covered
account linking and the remaining password flow. Production shows both OAuth
buttons and reaches Google's sign-in screen, but a complete Google sign-in
on the live site still needs confirmation. A working redirect alone doesn't
prove the callback and token exchange succeed.
